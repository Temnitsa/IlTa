## Structural gaps/overlaps (all 6 themes)

### Coverage gaps (vs EGU / Grammar for IELTS preposition syllabus)

1. **before / after, from … to, till (time)** — theme 1 teaches at/on/in, by/until/for/since/during but not before/after (EGU has a dedicated “before/after/until/by” unit), “from 9 to 5” and the informal “till”. No rule section and no target question; “before/after” appear only inside other sentences.
2. **Noun + preposition** — no theme covers it (reason for, solution to, advantage of, damage to, increase in, effect on, difference between). EGU and Grammar for IELTS both have a noun + preposition unit. Themes 4/5 cover adjective + preposition and verb + preposition only, theme 6 covers fixed phrases.
3. **like vs as** — absent (like = similar to; as = in the role of; such as). A classic EGU unit and a frequent IELTS error; no rule and no question.
4. **against / without** — no target coverage (against the wall, against the rules; without sugar, without a doubt); both appear only in the surrounding text. Basic prepositions that are not taught anywhere.
5. **while** — tested by match prep4-0078 without being taught (see major row).
6. **Prepositions at the end of relative clauses** — theme 6 §5 covers direct questions (“Who are you waiting for?”) but not “the man I spoke to / the book I told you about”.
7. **by + agent in passive; get in/on transport** — by-agent appears only inside one explanation (prep4-0095); get on the bus / get in the car vs by bus / by car is not taught even though theme 6 §3 says “on the train / in the car” in an explanation.
8. **in/on + corner, middle; at home / at work fixed phrases** — theme 2 questions test “in the corner of the room”, “on the corner”, “in the middle of the room” (prep1-0078, 0085, 0087) and “at home / at work” (prep1-0045, 0098), but rule §5 teaches only left/right, top/bottom and inside/outside — the rule section needs corner/middle and the fixed place phrases (rule-level gap in theme 2, for part A).

### Overlaps (same content taught in more than one theme)

1. **during / for — themes 1 §4 and 6 §2.** Theme 1 §4 already explains during (= period/event) and for (= duration); theme 6 §2 repeats the same definitions with new examples. The learner meets the same rule twice in two different themes.
2. **between / among — themes 2 §3 and 6 §4.** Both teach the pair; the definitions are not the same: theme 2 says “between ставим между двумя объектами, among — среди многих”, theme 6 says “between — два предмета или чётко разделённые группы, among — неопределённая группа”. The theme-2 simplification contradicts the more accurate theme-6 wording (between with clearly separated groups).
3. **over / under — themes 2 §2 and 3 §3.** Both themes teach the same prepositions; theme 2 says “over и under часто предполагают движение, укрытие или непосредственную близость” and its examples “over the bridge / under the bed” mix static and movement senses, while theme 3 handles over/under as movement with examples “over the fence / under the bridge”. The place/movement division for over/under is fuzzy and the examples nearly duplicate each other.
4. **for — themes 1 §4 and 6 §2** (duration) plus theme 5 §2 (for = reason/purpose after verbs): same preposition, three themes, no cross-references; the duration sense is taught twice.
5. **by — themes 1 §4 (deadline), 6 §3 (transport) and 6 §5 (by the time)**: different senses of the same preposition spread over three sections without cross-reference.
6. **Fixed expressions — themes 6 §1 vs 5 §2/§5 and 1 §1/2 §5.** Theme 6 is the designated “fixed expressions” theme, yet theme 5 teaches look for / ask for / take care of as fixed phrases, theme 1 fixes at night / at the weekend / at Christmas, theme 2 fixes at home / on the left. The same concept is spread over four themes with no link from theme 6 §1 to the others.
7. **in the end / at the end — theme 6 §1 vs §5** (internal overlap): “in the end” is listed as a fixed §1 phrase, “at the end of the lesson” appears only in §5 examples, and the contrast between them is never stated in the rule (explained only in question explanations).
8. **Examples repeated across sections of one rule** — prep-adjectives §1 vs §3/§4 (afraid of, good at) and prep-verbs §1 vs §2/§3 (depend on, look at, listen to): each rule re-uses collocations instead of showing a new pair.

### Progression A1–B2

The order is logical: themes 1–2 (A1: at/on/in time and place basics) → theme 3 (A2: movement) → themes 4–5 (A2: dependent prepositions after adjectives and verbs) → theme 6 (fixed expressions and contrasts). This matches the EGU-level order and is a sensible spiral. Two problems: (a) the sequence **ends at B1** even though the merged theme 6 contains 30 B2 questions and one of the old rules was explicitly B2 — the profile should end at B2 (see major row); (b) theme 1's new items on by/until/for/since/during are all tagged A1, although the surrounding present-perfect structures (for/since) are A2-level — consider A2 tags for those items (part A's file). Themes 4/5 keep the majority at their rule level (44/50 A2 each) ✓, themes 1–3 also ✓ (A1 42/50, A1 44/50, A2 44/50).

## Summary

**Critical ids: none** — the 40 new questions in themes 4–6 all have a unique defensible preposition with their current options, correct English, natural translations and correct explanations.

**Main structural gaps:** (1) **noun + preposition** is not covered at all; (2) the time theme lacks **before/after, from … to, till**; (3) **like vs as** and the basic prepositions **against / without** are missing; (4) **while** is tested but not taught; (5) prepositions at the end of relative clauses, **by + agent in passive** and **get in/on transport** are not taught; (6) theme 2's rule doesn't teach corner/middle and at home/at work though questions test them; (7) the merged theme 6's level label (B1) must become B2 (or be split back) — the progression then covers A1→B2 as intended. **Main overlaps:** during/for (themes 1/6), between/among (2/6, with inconsistent wording), over/under (2/3), for and by across three themes, fixed expressions scattered over themes 1/2/5/6 — plus the rule-level omissions in theme 6 (in the end/at the end contrast and while).
