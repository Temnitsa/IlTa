## Structural gaps/overlaps (all 9 themes)

### Coverage gaps (what EGU / Grammar for IELTS teaches but the 9 themes do not)

1. **Generic “the + singular”** (“The tiger is a dangerous animal”, “The computer has changed our lives”). The set has generic a/an (theme 7 §3) and generic zero (themes 1 §2 / 5 §1 / 9 §2), but not the third generic pattern with **the + singular**. This is also the root of the earlier a/an-generic ambiguities: without the “the + singular class” rule, items like “An honest politician is hard to find.” look as if “The honest politician…” were wrong. → add a section (theme 7 or 9): three generic patterns compared: The tiger / A tiger / Tigers.
2. **the + comparative** (“The more you practise, the better you become”; “the sooner, the better”) — absent from all 9 themes and questions.
3. **Names with and without the beyond geography/newspapers**: streets and squares (Oxford Street, Trafalgar Square), stations and airports (Victoria Station, Heathrow), universities (Oxford University vs the University of Oxford), churches/cathedrals (St Paul’s Cathedral), landmarks (the Eiffel Tower vs Buckingham Palace), companies/airlines, ships. Theme 6 §4 covers only newspapers/hotels/museums/theatres/organisations; “Mill Street” appears once (theme 7 match def-0109 right) without any rule.
4. **the + decades / eras / ages**: “the 1990s”, “the Middle Ages”, “in his thirties”. “the Renaissance” appears only inside an of-phrase (theme 9 adv-0070) with no rule behind it.
5. **the + family names**: “the Smiths”, “the Browns” — absent.
6. **the + body parts**: “hit him on the head”, “by the hand” — absent.
7. **the radio vs zero TV**: TV is covered (theme 5 §5 “watch TV”), the classic contrast “listen to the radio / watch TV” is absent; “the news”, “the beach”, “the mountains”, “the government” occur only in example sentences/match rights, never in a rule.
8. **Seasons**: “in summer” / “in the summer” — the pattern occurs 10 times in questions/matches (art2-0021, zero-0084, zero-0111), but theme 5 §4 mentions only time of day, days, months and holidays; no rule and no contrast for seasons.
9. **Exclamations**: “What a beautiful day!”, “such a…” — theme 4 §3 covers half/such/quite word order, but the “What a + adjective + noun!” pattern is absent.
10. **some/any + countable plural vs a/an**: theme 1 mentions “some water” in a §1 example only; the a/an ↔ some/any plural-meaning contrast (“I need an apple / some apples”) is not taught, although it is the first determiner unit in EGU after a/an.

Positive side: the 9 themes together do cover the bulk of the article syllabus — countable/uncountable, a/an (first mention, professions/nationalities/religions, sound rules, measures/frequency, half/such/quite, a/an vs one), the (shared knowledge, second mention, unique, of-phrases/relative clauses, superlatives/ordinals/only/same/next/last, institutions and leisure places), zero (general plural/uncountable, institutions, transport, meals/calendar/fixed expressions), proper names and geography, the + adjective and nationality names, fixed expressions with the/a/an, few/little, abstract nouns, and an academic/IELTS block.

### Overlaps (sections that teach the same thing again)

1. **Superlatives/ordinals/only/same/next/last — 3 sections**: theme 3 §4 (A2), theme 7 §5 (B2), theme 9 §4 (B2); themes 7 and 9 even share the example “This is the first day of spring.”, and the wording is contradictory (“обычно” vs “обязателен… другой артикль не ставится” — see major row). Recommendation: full rule in theme 3; short B2 reminders in themes 7/9 only (no duplicate sections).
2. **Of-phrase/relative-clause specification — 2 sections**: theme 3 §3 (A2) and theme 9 §3 (B2); 14 A2 questions (def-0039–def-0052) and art2-0014 plus 10 A2 questions def-0143–def-0152 sit in theme 9, which turns half of the “academic” theme into a copy of theme 3.
3. **Newspapers/hotels/museums + unique roles — 3 sections**: theme 2 §4 (A1!), theme 6 §4–§5, theme 8 §5; duplicated examples “the Ritz”, “the Louvre”, “the Pope”, “take the place of” between themes 2 and 6 (see major row).
4. **Fixed expressions — 4 sections**: theme 2 §4 (“take the place of”, “in front of”), theme 5 §4–§5, theme 6 §5 (“take place”, “take the place of”, “in front of/in the front of”), theme 8 §1–§2 and §4 — the same expression sets are taught four times.
5. **General/zero meaning — 3 sections**: theme 1 §2, theme 5 §1, theme 9 §1–§2; theme 1 and theme 5 share the example “Water is essential for life.” verbatim.
6. **the + known/specific — 3 introductions**: theme 1 §3, theme 3 §1–§2, theme 9 §1.
7. **Institutions + places — 2 sections**: theme 5 §2 (zero for the activity) and theme 7 §4 (the for the building/leisure places) — complementary, but the same words (school/hospital/cinema/theatre) are taught twice in two different themes and the same questions/patterns are re-used (e.g. theme 5 zero-0060/0072/art2-0022 vs theme 7 def-0068/0071/0073/0165/0166/0170).
8. **a/an — 3 themes**: theme 2 (first mention/professions), theme 4 (measures/sound), theme 7 §3 (generic) plus the misplaced first-mention items in theme 7. This is a spiral, but the a/an generic section is currently only match-tested (adv-0053/adv-0089), and theme 7 carries extra first-mention items that have no rule (critical row).

Duplicate rule examples across themes (in addition to the critical row): “Water is essential for life.” (theme 1 §2 = theme 5 §1), “the Ritz” and “the Louvre” (theme 2 §4 = theme 6 §4), “the Pope” and “take the place of” (theme 2 §3/§4 = theme 6 §5), “This is the first day of spring.” (theme 7 §5 = theme 9 §4). These are direct evidence of the overlaps above.

### Level progression A1→B2

The declared order is monotone and logical on paper: themes 1–2 = A1, 3–5 = A2, 6 = B1, 7–9 = B2. But the inherited question banks do not match the declared theme levels: theme 3 (A2) is 84% A1 (59/70); theme 7 (B2) is 43% B1 + 14% A2; theme 8 (B2) is 86% B1 (60/70); theme 9 (B2) is 57% A2 (40/70); theme 2 (A1) contains 10 B2 items. So difficulty rises much less than the labels suggest — the “B2” half of the course is mostly A2/B1 material with a B2 label. Recommendation: either set every theme’s `level` to the dominant level of its questions (3→A1, 7→B1/B2, 8→B1), or make themes 7–9 genuinely B2 by replacing recycled A2/B1 items with B2 work (generic the, the + comparative, error correction for theme 9).

## Summary
