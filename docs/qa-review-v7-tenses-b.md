### Coverage gaps (vs EGU / Grammar for IELTS)

1. **be going to — completely absent** from the whole section (0 occurrences). It is the main future form for plans/intentions and evidence-based predictions (“Look at those clouds — it’s going to rain!”) and the required contrast partner of will. Theme 9 even uses the classic going-to context with will (item tense-future-simple-0009 “Look at those clouds! It ___ rain.”). → add a theme or a section (plans/intentions/evidence) and contrast it with will.
2. **No consolidated future-forms overview.** Present Simple for timetables (theme 1 §2), Present Continuous for arrangements (theme 2 §2), will (theme 9), Future Continuous (theme 10) — but no section compares them, and there is no will vs going to vs present continuous vs present simple system. → add a summary/contrast section (e.g. in theme 9).
3. **used to / would for past habits** — absent (EGU unit 18; frequent IELTS topic). Theme 5 match 0030 labels “Привычка в прошлом” with “Every summer we spent a week by the sea.” — that is a repeated past action, but the dedicated form is not taught.
4. **Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous** — no contrast section; theme 4 mentions state verbs and “другое значение” but never compares hас been doing vs have done (the core EGU unit). The contrast is only implicit in match 0029.
5. **Present Simple vs Present Continuous** — no rule contrast in themes 1/2 (only one match in theme 2, 1 tagged contrast item). The classic “I do vs I am doing” unit is split and untested.
6. **Passive forms across tenses** — only Past Perfect passive appears (tense-past-perfect-0018, match 0027) and without any rule; Present Perfect/ Past Simple/ Future passives are absent. Either add a short passive note per theme or remove the unmatched item.
7. **Reported speech backshift** — Past Perfect items test it (theme 7: 0005, 0012, 0017; match 0026) but no rule covers reported speech; a note is needed (see major row).
8. **shall / be about to / be due to / have got / future in the past (was going to)** — all absent; acceptable for a 12-tense course, but worth at least one mention in the future theme(s).
9. **Time clauses beyond when/if/before/after** — theme 9 §4 covers exactly these four; as soon as, until, by the time + present, and “when I’ve finished” (present perfect in a time clause, EGU “when I do / when I’ve done”) are not covered. “by the time + present” appears only in themes 11/12 for the perfect future.

### Overlaps (same content taught in more than one theme)

1. **for / since** — themes 3 §3, 4 §3, 8 §5, 12 §3: four separate teachings of the same markers; each theme needs them, but the rule wording is almost identical and learners meet the same explanation four times (the cross-rule duplicate example “I have known her for ten years.” is part of this).
2. **by the time** — themes 7 §3, 11 §3, 12 §3: the marker is explained three times with the same “clause takes the present tense” note.
3. **State verbs** — themes 1 §4, 2 §4, 4 §4, 6 §4, 12 §4: five separate stative-verb notes; consistent, but one shared note with cross-references would avoid repetition.
4. **Time clauses whose verb is present** — themes 9 §4 (when/if/before/after) and 10 §3 (when/while) teach the same principle twice.
5. **“Duration up to a point”** — themes 4 §2, 8 §2, 12 §2 have near-identical wording for the present/past/future perfect continuous; parallel structure is justified, but the sections could cross-reference instead of repeating.
6. **Third form / participles** — themes 3, 5, 7, 8, 11 each re-teach the participle/third-form idea as part of the form section (natural spiral, but note 5 repetitions).
7. **Future meaning in present-tense themes** — themes 1 §2 (timetable) and 2 §2 (arrangements) teach future uses of present tenses, while themes 9–10 teach the future tenses; the same phenomenon is described in four places without one comparison (see gap 2).
8. **Match templates** — themes 7–9 repeat the same generic labels (“утвердительная форма / отрицательная форма / вопрос”) three times, and themes 10–12 repeat an identical four-label set in their forms matches; variety is low, and combined with the rule-example duplication (critical row) the matches are trivially solvable.

### Progression A1→B2

Per-theme levels match the difficulty of the tense (EGU-scale): Present Simple A1, Present Continuous A1, Present Perfect A2, Present Perfect Continuous B1, Past Simple A1, Past Continuous A2, Past Perfect B1, Past Perfect Continuous B2, Future Simple A1, Future Continuous B1, Future Perfect B2, Future Perfect Continuous B2. Within each family the progression is strictly rising (present A1→B1, past A1→B2, future A1→B2), which is exactly right. The overall number sequence resets to A1 when the family changes (1–4 present, 5–8 past, 9–12 future) — logical for a reference grouped by tense family (as the section description says), but if the UI sorts themes only by `order`, the difficulty dips three times; either keep the family grouping with an explicit family heading or sort within families by level. All 30 questions per theme share the theme level (no intra-theme gradation, acceptable for drills).

## Summary

**Critical id:** rule-example duplication (answer reveal) in themes 7–12 — the affected ids are listed in the first table row (e.g. `tense-past-perfect-0026`, `tense-past-perfect-continuous-0024`, `tense-future-continuous-0023/0024/0026/0028`, `tense-future-perfect-0008/0021/0022/0026/0029`, `tense-future-perfect-continuous-0021/0027`, `tense-future-simple-0029`); the same pattern exists in themes 1–6 (part A).

**Main structural gaps:** (1) **be going to** is missing from the whole course, and there is no consolidated future-forms contrast (will / going to / present continuous / present simple); (2) **used to / would** for past habits is missing; (3) **Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous** and **Present Simple vs Present Continuous** have no contrast sections; (4) passive forms and reported-speech backshift are tested once without any rule; (5) time clauses are limited to when/if/before/after (+ when/while), missing as soon as / until / present perfect in time clauses; (6) shall, be about to, have got, future in the past are absent (minor). **Main overlaps:** for/since ×4 themes, by the time ×3 themes, state-verb notes ×5 themes, time-clause rule ×2 themes, and future meaning split over themes 1/2/9/10; the match questions in themes 7–12 also reuse rule examples and repeat identical label sets.
