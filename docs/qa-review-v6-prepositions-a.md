## Structural gaps and overlaps involving themes 1–3

Coverage gaps (vs EGU / Grammar for IELTS; part B’s 6-theme audit lists the global set — the points that concern part A are repeated here with part-A actions):

1. **before / after / till + from … to (time)** — theme 1 teaches at/on/in and by/until/for/since/during but not before/after (EGU “before/after/until/by”), “from 9 to 5”, informal “till”, or from … to + time; the only from … to time question (`prep2-0038`) sits in the movement theme (see minor row). Add a short §3 note and, if desired, one time question (up to 60 questions are allowed).
2. **against / without** — no preposition theme covers them (they occur only inside other sentences); global gap, no theme to attach to.
3. **corner / middle / end / at home / at work / compass points** — theme 2 rule gap (major row above).
4. **like vs as, noun + preposition** — not covered by any theme (global gap; part B lists them).

Overlaps (same content in more than one theme):

1. **during / for** — theme 1 §3 and theme 6 §2 teach the same definitions twice (plus theme 5 §2 for the reason sense). Consider trimming theme 6 §2 to a reminder or adding a cross-reference.
2. **between / among** — theme 2 §2 says “between — два объекта, among — среди многих”, while theme 6 §4 correctly says “between — два предмета или чётко разделённые группы”; the theme-2 simplification contradicts the more accurate theme-6 wording. Align theme 2 (add «или чётко разделённые группы»).
3. **over / under** — theme 2 §1 (place) and theme 3 §2 (movement) both teach the pair; place examples “over the bridge / under the bed” mix static and movement senses and nearly duplicate movement examples “over the fence / under the bridge”; the same phrase also appears in questions (`prep1-0204` “climbed over the garden wall” ~ `prep2-0054` “cat climbed over the garden wall”, 0.66). Define place = static position, movement = path, and adjust examples/questions accordingly.
4. **by** — theme 1 §3 (deadline), theme 6 §3 (transport) and theme 6 §5 (by the time): different senses of by are spread over three sections without cross-references.
5. **fixed phrases** — theme 1 §0 (at night / at the weekend / at Christmas), theme 2 questions (at home / at work; rule gap) and theme 6 §1 (the designated fixed-expressions theme) spread the same concept over three themes; add links from theme 6 §1 to the earlier fixed phrases.

## Summary

The three themes cover the EGU/Grammar for IELTS scope for time, place and movement well: at/on/in, by/until/for/since/during, at/on/in place + above/below/over/under + between/among/opposite/by/beside + in front of/behind/next to + inside/outside/parts, and to/into/onto/out of/off/through/across/along/over/under/towards/past/around/round/from … to. All 60 new questions have correct keys, clean English and natural translations; the unique-answer check found **no critical ambiguity** — the only preposition-uniqueness risks were weak enough not to flag (`through` in prep2-0065, `over` in prep2-0069, `next to` in prep1-0210 are ruled out by the context; “on Monday at the latest” in ptime-0001/0002 is non-standard for a deadline). The substantive problems are: the duplicated tag arrays (systematic, functional impact on mistake review), the place-rule gap (corner/middle/at home/at work tested but not taught), the missing at/on holiday distinction in the time rule, one false explanation (ptime-0015), the near-duplicate answer reveal with theme 6 (prep1-0105 / prep4-0079), plus eight near-duplicates, one misplaced-question pair, the from … to time gap and the options/level inconsistencies.
